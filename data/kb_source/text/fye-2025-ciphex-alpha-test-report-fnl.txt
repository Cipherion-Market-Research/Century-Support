Scope and Data Basis 
This FYE 2025 report incorporates results from two testing periods: (1)  May 25 (inception) through 
November 30, 2025, and (2)  December 1 through December 31, 2025. Where relevant, November 2025 
performance is benchmarked against Bitcoin and Ethereum using historical snapshot pricing from 
CoinMarketCap, and against traditional risk assets using published total return series for the S&P 500 and 
the Nasdaq 100. 
Headline Results for FYE 2025 
As of December 31, 2025, the CipheX Abacus AMS and EMS system (CipheX Alpha) delivered a total return 
since inception of 9.96%, with a maximum drawdown of 11.46%  and a Capital Return Efficiency (CRE) of 
0.87. Risk-adjusted returns since inception stand at 5.78%, indicating that volatility, drawdown depth, and 
execution costs meaningfully reduce retained performance. At this early stage  of optimization, the system 
demonstrated a credible performance edge, while requiring further refinement in risk calibration and 
execution efficiency to achieve scalable, risk-normalized results suitable for commercial deployment. 
Period Performance and Return Path 
In 2025, a crypto -led drawdown in November was followed by stabilization and a partial recovery in 
December. The Abacus system declined 6.28%  from November 1 to November 30 and recovered 3.02%  
in December, maintaining controlled capital deployment through the correction and into year -end. 
Importantly, Abacus is not designed to operate as a traditional investment fund, and its reported AUM 
reflects an internal optimization phase rather than deployed third-party capital. Industry research published 
by CoinShares shows that early-stage crypto hedge funds and trading platforms with comparable or smaller 
capital bases frequently experienced losses of approximately 15% or more  during similar market 
corrections, indicating that AUM size alone does not explain downside containment. In this context, the 
system’s November performance reflects a genuine execution and risk -management edge driven by its 
price-predictive and exposure -control framework, rather than reduced market impact associated with 
lower AUM. 
Drawdown Containment and Capital Preservation 
During November, the system declined 6.28%, as AUM decreased from approximately $307 thousand at 
the start of the month to about $288 thousand  by month-end. The maximum peak -to-trough drawdown 
during the period reached 11.46%, as AUM moved from a high near $310 thousand  to a low around $275 
thousand. This drawdown represents the primary constraint on full- year performance and results in a 
Capital Return Efficiency (CRE) of 0.87. A CRE below 1.0 indicates that cumulative returns have not yet 
exceeded the system’s largest drawdown, highlighting the need for continued refinement of drawdown 
controls rather than a structural weakness in the strategy.  
As volatility expanded and short- horizon stability deteriorated, the system identified conditions in which 
loss absorption was preferable to continued exposure.  Capital-at-risk was reduced through compressed 
position sizing and a coordinated derisking of aggregate exposure. Capital preservation preceded selective 
redeployment into lower- beta structures, during which correlation to core crypto benchmarks declined. 
These defensive adjustments were executed through the system’s price -predictive framework and 
operated in a coordinated manner across strategies.

This integrated response limited the propagation of adverse price movement before drawdowns could 
compound. The controlled nature of the November drawdown preserved capital and execution capacity, 
enabling participation in the December recovery without req uiring an aggressive increase in exposure as 
market conditions stabilized. This was not a full transition to cash, but a measured contraction of risk that 
preserved optionality. From an institutional risk perspective, the system prioritized loss containment over 
trade duration, demonstrating disciplined risk governance under stressed market conditions. 
November 2025 Crypto Correction Comparables 
November 2025 represented a crypto -specific market correction, making Bitcoin and Ethereum the most 
relevant benchmarks. Based on CoinMarketCap historical snapshots, Bitcoin declined from approximately 
$110,064 on November 1 to $90,394 by November 30, a drawdown of roughly 17.9%. Over the same 
period, Ethereum fell to approximately $2,992, representing a decline of about 22.8%. In contrast, 
traditional markets remained comparatively stable throughout November. YCharts reports that the S&P 500 
delivered an approximate monthly total return of +0.25%, while the Nasdaq 100 Total Return Index declined 
by 1.57%, according to data published by Nasdaq (Nasdaq Global Index Watch). Against this backdrop, the 
Abacus system demonstrated effective downside containment relative to core crypto benchmarks , 
indicating its risk controls and exposure management operated as intended.

December 2025 Recovery Quality and Efficiency 
December delivered a 3.02% gain , moving the system back toward prior equity levels and confirming its 
ability to sustain market engagement both during stress and in the aftermath of a drawdown. For testing 
purposes, risk-adjusted returns are calculated by subtracting the prevailing US 10-year Treasury Note yield 
at each valuation point, isolating performance generated above a risk-free benchmark. Since inception, this 
methodology produces a 5.78% risk-adjusted return versus a 9.96% total return, indicating that volatility 
exposure, timing dispersion, and execution costs materially reduced retained performance. This spread 
between total and risk-adjusted returns represents a core focus of ongoing system optimization rather than 
a degradation of underlying signal quality.

Execution Friction and Transaction Costs 
The system attempts to separate trade-level fees and slippage by exchange venue, making execution costs 
easier to diagnose where venue-level data is available. In crypto markets, these costs can fluctuate widely, 
and fee disclosures vary across venues with no consistent reporting standards. December data show that 
combined trade fees, slippage, and related transaction costs ranged from approximately 0.6 0% to around 
2.0%, materially reducing net performance. Accordingly, improving execution efficiency remain s a core 
optimization priority at this stage, with the objective of enhancing returns without increasing market 
exposure. 
Conclusion for FYE 2025 
From an institutional perspective, the FYE 2025 results confirm that the system functions as a risk-managed 
capital engine rather than a pro -cyclical trading vehicle. Down- market alpha in November was generated 
through structural risk discipline rather tha n directional prediction. FYE 2025 demonstrated credible 
downside containment during a meaningful crypto correction, supported by November Bitcoin and 
Ethereum pricing from CoinMarketCap  historical snapshots. The primary constraint at this stage is 
efficiency rather than edge, as maximum drawdown remains high relative to total return. Capital Return 
Efficiency (CRE) remains below 1.0, making risk-adjusted performance and improved risk-state calibration 
an optimization priority during precommercial testing. As these optimization efforts progress, the existing 
performance profile has a clear path to higher CRE and stronger risk- adjusted results without increasing 
directional market exposure. 
FY2026 Phase III of V Optimization Requirements 
As system optimization continues, Abacus deliberately maintains higher trading intervals to support deeper 
signal analysis, isolate execution behavior, and evaluate optimization outcomes through internal expert 
contributors. These initiatives focus on impr oving execution quality and strengthening downside controls 
across varying market conditions. In parallel, strategy -based rules are refined to prioritize risk reduction 
during sudden or prolonged stress events, with position reentry treated as a deliberate  strategic process 
rather than a linear profit and loss response or conventional modeling assumption. 
The objective of Optimization Phase III is to enhance adaptive risk awareness, gradually reduce trading 
frequency through higher-confidence setups and improved target identification logic, and decrease manual 
oversight as automation confidence increases. C ollectively, these improvements are intended to increase 
probability consistency and return quality across multiple asset classes and market environments.
